## Part 15 — The Russian Prince's Advice

In Strasbourg, Julien had to wait a week for the answer to his secret message. He was very unhappy. He thought only of Mathilde.

One day, in the street, he met the Russian Prince Korasoff, whom he had met in London some months before, during a trip there for the Marquis. The prince was a young, elegant, cheerful man who knew everything about love and women. He saw at once that Julien was suffering.

"My dear friend," he said, "you are in love. I can see it. And the woman is treating you badly. Tell me everything."

Julien told him the whole story, without saying Mathilde's name.

The prince laughed. "Oh, it is very simple," he said. "This young lady is proud. She loves you, but she wants to control you. As long as she knows that you love her, she will torture you. You must make her think that you do not love her any more. You must make her jealous. Choose another woman in her circle, a woman of high society, and court her. Pay her compliments. Write her love letters. Be very attentive to her — and completely cold to the young lady. You will see, she will come back to you on her knees."

"But I don't know how to write love letters," said Julien.

"Don't worry," laughed the prince. "I have a collection. Fifty-three love letters, written by a friend of mine to a very pious, very proper lady. They are perfect. You just have to copy them, one each day, and send them. Choose a lady who is religious and serious. They are the easiest to impress with these letters."

**Madame de Fervaques**

When Julien returned to Paris, he followed the prince's advice. He chose the Maréchale de Fervaques, a rich, beautiful widow who often came to the de La Mole house. She was very religious and very proud of her noble family. She was a little stiff and serious, and nobody had ever dared to court her.

Every evening, in the drawing room, Julien sat near Madame de Fervaques and talked to her with great attention. He told her how much he admired her virtue and her religious feelings. He did not look at Mathilde once. And every day, he copied one of the prince's letters and sent it to Madame de Fervaques.

It was very hard. The letters were long and boring, full of religious ideas and old-fashioned compliments. Sometimes Julien was so tired and unhappy that he made mistakes when he copied them. Once, he forgot to change the name of the city in the letter, and wrote "London" instead of "Paris". But Madame de Fervaques did not notice. She was flattered. Nobody had ever written such letters to her. Slowly, she began to answer them.

**Mathilde's Jealousy**

Mathilde watched all this. At first, she laughed at it. "He is courting that old prude? How ridiculous!" But as the weeks passed, she began to suffer. Julien never looked at her. He was always with Madame de Fervaques. He was cold and polite to her, like a stranger.

"He doesn't love me any more," she thought. "He loves that woman. I have lost him." And for the first time in her life, Mathilde de La Mole was unhappy because of a man. Her pride was broken.

One day, she found a pile of letters from Madame de Fervaques on Julien's desk, unopened. She could not stand it any longer. She went to him, pale and crying, and she fell on her knees in front of him.

"Julien," she said, "you despise me! I deserve it. But I love you. I can't live without you. I am your slave. Punish me, if you like, but don't leave me!"

Julien felt a wild joy. He had won! But he remembered the prince's advice. "If I show her how much I love her," he thought, "she will become cold and proud again. I must hide my feelings." So, although he wanted to take her in his arms, he spoke to her calmly, almost coldly.

It was the hardest thing he had ever done. But it worked. Mathilde loved him more than ever, because she was afraid of losing him. She obeyed him. She looked at him with respect and admiration. Her love became humble and passionate. Julien was happy at last — but he was always afraid. He knew that if he ever relaxed, if he ever let her see his true feelings, she would become proud and cruel again.

**The News**

A few months later, Mathilde told Julien a great secret. She was going to have a baby.

Julien was frightened. "Your father will kill me," he said.

But Mathilde was happy and proud. "Now we must be married," she said. "I will be your wife. I will tell my father everything. And if he refuses, I will leave his house and live with you, even in poverty."

She wrote a long letter to her father, telling him the truth.
