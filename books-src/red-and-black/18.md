## Part 18 — Mathilde in Besançon

When Mathilde heard the news, she left Paris at once, without telling her father. She came to Besançon in disguise, wearing simple clothes and a big hat, under a false name. She bribed the jailers and came to see Julien in his prison.

She threw herself into his arms. "Julien!" she cried. "I am here! I will save you! I will do everything — everything!"

Julien was moved by her courage. She had left her family, her reputation, her comfortable life, to come to him. She was like Queen Marguerite, who had risked everything for Boniface de La Mole.

Mathilde was excited. She thought her situation was very romantic. She worked day and night to save him. She went to see the judges, the lawyers, and the important people of Besançon. She spent a lot of money. She even went to see a powerful priest, Father de Frilair, who controlled many people in the city. He promised to help — if she could get a favour for him from her family in Paris.

"I will get you out of here," she said to Julien every day. "We will escape to America, or to Italy. We will be free."

**Julien's Thoughts**

But Julien was tired of her heroism. She talked so much, she was so excited, she was always acting a part. He admired her, but he did not love her. Every day, he thought more and more of Madame de Rênal — of her gentle face, her simple love, the happy days at Vergy, the lime tree in the garden, the walks in the woods.

"Mathilde loves me with her head," he thought. "She loves the drama of my situation. But Madame de Rênal loved me with her heart. She loved me for myself. And I have tried to kill her."

He began to see his life clearly, perhaps for the first time. He had been so busy with his ambition, so busy pretending and calculating, that he had never really lived. He had chased power and success, and he had not seen the happiness that was right in front of him.

"I was happy at Vergy," he said to himself, "and I did not know it. Ambition carried me away to imaginary lands. Now that I am going to die, I understand."

**Fouqué**

His friend Fouqué came to see him too. Fouqué was a simple man, but he had a big heart. He wanted to sell everything he owned to bribe the jailers and help Julien escape. Julien was moved to tears.

"No, my friend," he said. "Keep your money. I don't want to escape. But thank you. You are the only true friend I have ever had."

**Death**

Julien thought a lot about death. He was not afraid of it. He had always admired brave men who died for their ideas, like Count Altamira, or like Boniface de La Mole. Now he would die too.

"What is death?" he said to himself. "Napoleon died on a small island in the middle of the ocean. Danton died on the guillotine. Every great man dies. It is not death that matters, but how one dies. I will die calmly, without fear, like a soldier."

He looked out of his window at the beautiful sky, and at the mountains, and he felt strangely peaceful.

The date of the trial came closer.
