## Part 14 — The Sword

The next morning, Julien expected Mathilde to be happy and tender. But when he saw her in the library, she was cold. She looked at him with anger in her eyes, as if she hated him.

Mathilde was ashamed. In the light of day, she regretted what she had done. "I have given myself to a man of no importance," she thought. "I, Mathilde de La Mole! I have given him a master's power over me. Now he thinks I belong to him. He will want to control me." Her pride could not bear it.

For several days, she did not speak to him. When she did, she was proud and cruel. She told him, in front of other people, that he was nothing.

Julien was in despair. He had thought that he did not love her. But now that she was rejecting him, he discovered that he did love her — passionately, madly. He could not eat, he could not sleep. He thought only of her. Her coldness made him desperate. He walked alone for hours in the garden.

**The Sword**

One day, they met in the library. Mathilde said to him, in a voice full of contempt:

"I am sorry I gave myself to the first person who came."

Julien felt a terrible pain. In a moment of madness, he looked around and saw an old sword hanging on the wall — a medieval sword, kept as a decoration. He took it down and pulled it out of its case. He raised it over Mathilde's head.

Mathilde did not move. She did not scream. She looked at him with shining eyes. She was not afraid; she was delighted. "He wanted to kill me!" she thought. "He is a real man, like the men of the sixteenth century! He is worthy of me!"

But Julien, seeing her look, put the sword down. He was ashamed. He hung it back on the wall and left the room.

That evening, Mathilde loved him again — more than ever. She was proud of having been loved so passionately, so dangerously. She wrote to him. That night, he climbed up the ladder to her room again, and they were happy together. She called him her master. She cut off a lock of her hair and gave it to him.

**Hot and Cold**

But the next day, she was cold again. And so it continued. One day she loved him, the next day she hated him. One day she was his slave, the next day she was his queen. Julien suffered terribly. He did not understand her. He only knew that he loved her and that she was torturing him.

He went to the opera and saw her there with the Marquis de Croisenois. She ignored him completely. He felt that he would die of pain.

**A Secret Mission**

During this time, the Marquis de La Mole gave Julien an important and secret job. One night, he took him to a house where a group of very powerful men had met — dukes, a cardinal, generals, and an important foreign minister. They were planning a political plot. They wanted foreign kings to send armies to France, to help the old nobility keep its power against the people.

Julien's job was to listen, to remember everything they said, and then to carry the message, by heart, to a powerful man in a foreign country. Nothing could be written down. The Marquis trusted Julien completely, because of his wonderful memory.

Julien listened to the conversation. He was disgusted. These men were ready to betray their own country to keep their privileges. But he did his job. He learned the whole message by heart. Then he left Paris at night and travelled by carriage across France to Strasbourg, and from there to the foreign country.

On the way, someone tried to stop him — spies, who knew that he was carrying a secret message. But Julien escaped. He delivered the message and waited for the answer.
