## Part 17 — The Church at Verrières

It was a Sunday morning when Julien arrived in Verrières. He did not go to his father's house. He did not speak to anyone. He went straight to a gun shop.

"I want a pair of pistols," he said to the gunsmith. "Load them for me."

The gunsmith loaded them. Julien paid and put them in his pocket. Then he walked to the church.

The bells were ringing. It was the time of the morning mass. The church was full of people. Julien went in. The tall windows were covered with red curtains, just as on the day he had found the piece of newspaper, many years before. In the red light, he saw Madame de Rênal. She was kneeling at her usual place, praying.

When he saw her, his arm shook so much that at first he could not do anything. "I can't," he thought. "I can't do it." Then the bell rang for the most sacred moment of the mass, and Madame de Rênal bent her head. For a moment, under her shawl, she looked almost exactly like the woman who had loved him so much.

Julien raised his pistol and fired. He missed. He fired again. Madame de Rênal fell.

**Arrest**

There was a terrible noise. People screamed and ran in all directions. Julien stood still. He did not try to escape. He did not understand what he had done. When the people ran out of the church, one of them pushed him, and he fell. Someone took his pistols. Then a policeman came and arrested him.

He was taken to the prison of Verrières. They put chains on his hands. He did not resist. He did not speak. He sat on his bed and stared at the wall. "It is finished," he thought. "I have done it. I will die, and I deserve it."

He slept deeply that night, like a man who has finished a long, hard piece of work.

**She Is Alive**

The next day, the jailer came into his cell. "Sir," he said, "I have news. Madame de Rênal is not dead. The bullet went into her shoulder. The doctor says that she will live."

Julien jumped up. "She is not dead?" he cried. And then he fell on his knees and began to cry — great, violent tears, like a child. "She will live! Thank God! Thank God!"

At that moment, for the first time in a long time, he believed in God. He prayed. He felt that a great weight had been lifted from his heart. He did not care about himself any more. He only cared that she was alive.

He also realised something else. All his anger had disappeared. He did not hate her. He understood that the letter was not really hers — that she had written it because she was religious and afraid, and because someone had pushed her to do it. And he understood that she was the only woman he had ever really loved.

**Prison**

Some days later, Julien was taken to Besançon, to the big prison in the old castle. He was given a room high up in a tower. From his window, he had a beautiful view of the city and the mountains.

He was calm, almost happy. He spent his days reading and thinking. He did not want to be saved. "I have tried to kill a woman," he said. "I deserve to die. Let them judge me."

The jailer was very kind to him. Julien soon discovered why: someone was sending him money every day to treat the young prisoner well. It was Madame de Rênal. She was not angry with him at all. She was secretly happy that he had tried to kill her, because it meant that he still loved her. As soon as she could get out of bed, she wrote to the jury members, and to the judges, and to the priests, begging them to save his life.

"If he dies," she thought, "I will die too."

But she did not visit him. Her husband would not allow it. And her confessor told her that it would be a terrible sin.
