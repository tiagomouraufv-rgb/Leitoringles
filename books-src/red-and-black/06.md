## Part 6 — The King's Visit

One day, a great piece of news arrived in Verrières: the King was going to visit the region! He would pass through Verrières on his way to pray at the famous church of Bray-le-Haut, where the bones of a holy saint were kept.

The whole town was excited. Everyone wanted to see the King and to be seen by him. The mayor decided that there would be a guard of honour: young men from the best families of the town, on horseback, in beautiful blue uniforms with silver decorations, to ride beside the King's carriage.

Madame de Rênal had an idea. She wanted Julien to be in the guard of honour. She knew how much he would love to wear a uniform and ride a horse like a soldier. She did everything she could to arrange it, and in the end she succeeded. Monsieur de Rênal agreed, because he thought it would show everyone how important his household was.

The other families of Verrières were furious. "What?" they said. "The son of a carpenter, riding with our sons, in front of the King? It is a scandal!" Monsieur Valenod was the most angry of all.

**The Uniform**

On the great day, Julien wore the blue uniform with silver buttons, and a tall hat with feathers. He rode a beautiful horse that belonged to a rich gentleman. He had never ridden a horse in front of so many people, and at first he was afraid. But he rode well, and he was very handsome in his uniform. The young women of the town looked at him and whispered. Madame de Rênal, standing at a window, watched him with pride and love.

When the King's carriage arrived, the crowd shouted, "Long live the King!" The guard of honour rode beside the carriage. For a few moments, Julien felt like a hero. He was a soldier! He was riding next to a king! "This is how it was with Napoleon's officers," he thought. His heart was full of joy.

But his horse got frightened by the noise of the crowd, and he almost fell off. Everyone laughed. Julien went red, but he stayed in the saddle.

**The Church**

After the procession, Julien quickly took off his uniform and put on his black clothes again. Father Chélan had asked him to help at the ceremony in the church of Bray-le-Haut, as an assistant to the priests.

The church was very beautiful, decorated with gold and flowers and hundreds of candles. A young bishop, the Bishop of Agde, had come to lead the ceremony. He was only about thirty years old, and he was handsome and elegant. Julien was surprised. "So young," he thought, "and already a bishop! And how much money does he earn?"

Before the ceremony, Julien found the young bishop alone in a room, practising in front of a mirror. He was giving blessings to the empty air with his hand, carefully, again and again, to make sure that his movements looked graceful and noble. Julien watched him, amazed. "This is what power is," he thought. "It is a performance, like the theatre."

During the ceremony, the King knelt in front of the saint's bones, and the bishop gave the blessing. All the people fell on their knees. The music was beautiful. Julien was moved in spite of himself. For a moment, he felt that he could become a priest, and even a bishop, if he played his part well.

But he had seen the bishop practising in front of the mirror, and he could not forget it.

**After the Visit**

After the King's visit, everybody in Verrières talked about Julien. Some admired him. Many were jealous. People began to say that he was too proud, too ambitious. "Who does he think he is?" they said. "The carpenter's son on a horse, in front of the King!"

Father Chélan was worried. He had heard the rumours, and he was afraid for Julien. "My son," he said, "be careful. You have enemies now. And I am afraid that your heart is not with God. Think about what you really want."

Julien listened politely, but he did not change. He was proud of his success. And he was happy, because Madame de Rênal loved him more than ever.

But in Verrières, everyone watches everyone. And Elisa, the maid whom Julien had refused to marry, was watching him very carefully.
