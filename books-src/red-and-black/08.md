## Part 8 — Besançon

From the top of a hill, Julien saw Besançon for the first time. It was a big city, with high walls and strong fortifications, built by the old kings. He thought it was one of the prettiest cities in France. "How I would love to be a soldier here, defending these walls!" he thought.

But he was not going to be a soldier. He was going to be a priest.

**The Café**

He arrived early in the morning and walked around the city. He was hungry, so he went into a big café. It was the first café he had ever seen, and it seemed very grand to him: there were big mirrors, marble tables and men playing billiards. The men looked at him and laughed at his country clothes.

At the counter sat a young woman. She was the cashier. She was pretty, with bright eyes and a kind smile. Julien was shy, but he spoke to her. He told her that he was going to the seminary. Her name was Amanda Binet. She was kind to him and gave him a cup of coffee and some bread.

"You're very young to become a priest," she said, looking at him with interest. "And very handsome."

They talked for a few minutes, while she was not busy. She told him that if he ever came back to the café, he could say that he was her cousin. She gave him her address. Then a big young man came in, who seemed to be her lover. He looked at Julien angrily, and Julien wanted to fight him. But Amanda begged him not to, and he left.

**The Seminary**

The seminary was a big, gloomy building near the cathedral. Over the door there was an iron cross. Julien rang the bell. After ten minutes, a pale man in black opened the door, looked at him without speaking, and led him through long, dark corridors to a small room.

There, behind a desk covered with papers, sat a man in an old black robe. He was very thin, with a face so pale and hard that it seemed to be made of stone. He did not look up for a long time. This was Father Pirard, the director of the seminary.

Julien was so frightened that he almost fainted. His legs were shaking. He had never felt such fear, not even when his father beat him. At last, Father Pirard looked up. His eyes were small and black and very sharp, like the eyes of a hunter.

"Come here," he said. "What is your name?"

"Julien Sorel, Father."

Father Pirard read the letter of recommendation from Father Chélan. Then he asked Julien questions in Latin about the Bible and about theology. Julien answered perfectly. Father Pirard was surprised. He asked more difficult questions. Julien answered them too. Then Father Pirard asked him about the Church Fathers — the great writers of the early Church. Julien knew them all.

At last, Father Pirard said: "Your answers are good. You have a good memory. But I see that you are proud. Pride is a great sin, my son. Here, you must learn to be humble. You will obey all the rules. You will not leave the seminary without my permission. You will not receive any letters without my reading them first. And you will have no friends. Do you understand?"

"Yes, Father."

"Father Chélan writes that you have no money. I will give you a scholarship. You will not pay anything. But I warn you: here, there are many who will hate you, because you are clever and because you are not like them. Be careful."

Then he called a servant, and Julien was taken to a small, cold cell on the top floor. There was a bed, a chair and a small window. From the window he could see the walls of the city, and beyond them the green hills and the river.

When he was alone, Julien sat on the bed and thought: "Here, I must be a hypocrite every minute of every day. Here, I must hide everything that I am. This is my battlefield. Napoleon fought with guns. I will fight with lies."

He did not know how hard it would be.
