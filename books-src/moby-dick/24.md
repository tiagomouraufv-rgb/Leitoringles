## Part 24 — The Doubloon and the Samuel Enderby

**The Doubloon**

The gold coin that Ahab had nailed to the main mast was still there, shining in the sun. Nobody touched it. It belonged to the man who would first see Moby Dick.

It was a Spanish coin from Ecuador, a country on the equator, on the west coast of South America. On it there were pictures of three mountains. On the first mountain there was a flame; on the second, a tower; on the third, a rooster crowing. Above them was part of the zodiac — the circle of the stars — with the sun entering the sign of Libra, the scales.

One morning Ahab stood in front of the coin and looked at it for a long time.

"There's something important in all things," he said, "or the world would mean nothing. Look at these mountains. They are proud, like Lucifer. The tower — that is Ahab. The volcano — that is Ahab. The brave, unafraid, victorious rooster — that is Ahab too. All are Ahab. This round gold is like the round world, which, like a magician's glass, shows each man only his own face."

When he went away, Starbuck came and looked at the coin. "I see three mountains," he said, "like the Trinity. And there is darkness in the valleys between them. But the sun of righteousness shines above them. Oh, Father, keep me from losing my faith!" And he went away quickly, afraid of his own thoughts.

Then Stubb came. He studied the zodiac and laughed. "Here is the story of a man's life, from spring to winter," he said, "and it ends in the fish sign. There's a sermon in these stars. Jolly, he's never sad! Ha, ha!"

Then Flask came, and he only said: "I see nothing here but a round thing made of gold. Sixteen dollars. At two cents a cigar, that's nine hundred and sixty cigars." And he went away.

The old Manxman came, and said that the white whale would be seen in the month of the sign on the coin. Queequeg came, and compared the signs with the tattoos on his body. Fedallah came, and bowed to the coin, because he worshipped fire and the sun.

Last of all, little Pip came, and he looked at the coin and said, "I look, you look, he looks; we look, you look, they look." He said this again and again. Then he said, "Here's the ship's navel. Here's her navel. And they're all burning to unscrew it. But unscrew your navel, and what happens? Your bottom falls off!" Poor mad Pip. Sometimes the mad say wise things.

**The Samuel Enderby**

Some weeks later, we met an English whaling ship, the *Samuel Enderby*, from London. Ahab shouted, "Have you seen the White Whale?"

The English captain held up his arm. It was not a real arm. It was made of white whalebone, like Ahab's leg. "Do you see this?" he cried.

Ahab was so excited that he ordered his boat to be lowered at once, and he was rowed over to the English ship. But he could not climb up the side with his ivory leg. The English captain understood, and he lowered a big hook from the ship. Ahab sat on it, and they pulled him up like a whale.

The English captain, whose name was Boomer, was a big, happy, friendly man. "Shake hands, sir," he said. "Bone to bone — an ivory arm and an ivory leg! Ha, ha!"

"Where did you see him?" asked Ahab. "How long ago?"

Captain Boomer told his story. He had seen Moby Dick on the equator, the season before. He did not know at first that it was the famous white whale. He saw a group of whales and lowered his boats. Then suddenly a huge white whale rose out of the water, with a milky white head and hump, and many old harpoons in his side. One of the harpoons caught the line, and the whale broke Boomer's boat in half with his tail. Captain Boomer tried to hold on to the harpoon, but the whale went down, and the harpoon cut his arm badly. The arm became infected, and the ship's doctor, Dr Bunger, had to cut it off.

"Did you ever meet him again?" asked Ahab.

"Twice," said Boomer. "But I didn't go after him. One arm is enough for me. He's welcome to the arm he has, since I can't help it. But he won't have the other. No more white whales for me. I lowered for him once, and that's enough. There would be great glory in killing him, I know; and there's a ship-load of precious oil in him. But he's best left alone. Don't you think so, Captain?"

"He is. But he will still be hunted, for all that," said Ahab. "Which way was he going?"

"Bless my soul!" said Boomer. "East, I think. Is your captain crazy?" he whispered to Fedallah.

But Ahab was already climbing down into his boat. "Back to the ship!" he shouted to his men. He was in such a hurry that, as he jumped into the boat, he hit his ivory leg against the side, and it was badly cracked.

**Measuring a Whale**

I have studied whales in many ways. I once saw the skeleton of a great sperm whale on an island in the South Pacific, in a beautiful green temple made of trees. The islanders worshipped it like a god. I measured it with a rope, and I had the measurements tattooed on my right arm, because I had no paper to write on. The skeleton was seventy-two feet long. A living whale of that size would be about ninety feet long.

**Will the Whales Disappear?**

Some people worry that so many whales are being killed that one day there will be none left, like the buffalo on the American prairies, which are being killed in their thousands. I do not think so. The whales are clever. They have learned to live in groups for protection, and to go to the coldest seas of the North and South Poles, where no ship can follow them. The whale existed before men, and I believe he will exist after them. The oceans are too large, and the whale is too strong. He will survive.
