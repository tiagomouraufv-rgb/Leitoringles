## Part 27 — The Harpoon and the Bachelor

**The Pacific**

At last we sailed into the great Pacific Ocean. For me, it was the answer to a hundred prayers I had made as a boy. This calm, wide ocean, which covers half the world, seems to hold the spirits of all the dead who lie in it. Its soft waves seem to whisper secrets. It rolls gently between the countries of Asia and the shores of America, and its islands are like the small, green dreams of a sleeping giant.

But Ahab did not feel any of this peace. To him, the Pacific was only the ocean where Moby Dick lived. Every day he looked at the sea, and every night he looked at his charts. We were getting closer to the place where he had decided to fight.

**The Blacksmith**

The blacksmith, Perth, was an old man with burned hands and a sad face. He had not always been a sailor. Once he had been a good blacksmith on land, with a house, a wife and three children. But he began to drink, and drink destroyed everything. He lost his business. His wife and children died, one after another. At last, alone and old, he decided to die too. But the sea called him. "Come to us," the sea seemed to say. "We have a new life for you, without the pain of death." So he became a whaleman, and now he worked at his little fire on the deck of the *Pequod*, without complaining, and without hope.

**The Forge**

One night, Ahab came to the blacksmith with a bag. "Perth," he said, "I want a harpoon made — a harpoon that a thousand pairs of devils' hands could not break."

He opened the bag. Inside were many small, bent pieces of steel. "These are the nail-stubs from the shoes of racehorses," Ahab said. "They are the toughest steel in the world. Make me a harpoon of them."

Perth heated the nails in his fire and began to hammer them together. Ahab watched. When the long iron head was ready, Ahab gave him his own razors. "And here are my razors," he said, "the best steel there is. Make the barbs of my harpoon from them. I will never shave again."

When the harpoon was nearly ready, it had to be dipped into liquid to harden the steel. "Water?" asked Perth.

"No, no," said Ahab. "Not water. I want it to be hardened with something better. Queequeg, Tashtego, Daggoo! Come here, you pagans! Will you give me as much blood as will cover this barb?"

The three harpooners nodded. Ahab cut their arms, and the steel was hardened in their blood. As it hissed and steamed, Ahab cried out in Latin, in a terrible voice: "I do not baptize you in the name of the Father, but in the name of the devil!"

Then he fixed the iron head onto a long pole of wood, and tied a new rope to it. He held the harpoon in the air and looked at it with a wild, fierce joy. Then he went to his cabin. Behind him, Pip laughed sadly.

**A Calm Sea**

Near the coast of Japan, the weather was beautiful, and the sea was calm and golden. The days seemed to last for ever. Even Ahab seemed to become a little gentler.

"Oh, grassy meadows! Oh, endless spring in the soul!" he said, standing on the deck. "I wish I could rest here. But no. Life is not a single, straight path, from child to man. It goes round and round in circles. First we are babies, then boys, then men full of doubts, then believers, then unbelievers, then again babies. Where is the final harbour, where we can rest? Where is the father of the lost child? Our souls are like orphans whose mothers died when they were born. We search for our fathers, and we never find them."

Starbuck, looking at the same calm sea, said: "Lovely sea, it makes me forget the terrible things under the surface. Let faith keep away the facts. Let fancy keep away memory."

And Stubb said: "I am Stubb, and Stubb has his history. But here Stubb swears that he has always been happy!"

**The Bachelor**

Soon afterwards we met a ship from Nantucket called the *Bachelor*. She was the happiest ship I ever saw. Her voyage had been a great success. She was full to the top with oil — every cask, every room, even the captain's cabin was full of barrels. She was going home.

On her deck, there was a party. The sailors were dancing with girls from the Polynesian islands. Her captain stood proudly on the quarter-deck, waving at us. A drum was beating, and the men were singing.

"Come on board, come on board!" shouted the happy captain. "Come and share our party!"

"Have you seen the White Whale?" Ahab shouted back.

"No! Only heard of him. But I don't believe in him at all," said the captain cheerfully. "Come on board!"

"You are too happy," said Ahab. "Sail on. Have you lost any men?"

"Not enough to talk about — two islanders, that's all. But come on board, old man, come along! I'll soon take that black from your face. We're full, and we're going home!"

"You are a full ship and going home, you say. Well, then, call me an empty ship, and going out. So go your way, and I'll go mine. Forward there! Set all sail, and keep her to the wind!"

And so the happy ship sailed home with a good wind behind her, while the *Pequod* sailed on, against the wind, towards her dark fate. As Ahab watched the *Bachelor* go, he took out a small bottle of sand from Nantucket and looked at it for a long time.
