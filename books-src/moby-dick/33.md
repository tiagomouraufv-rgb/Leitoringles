## Part 33 — The Chase: The Third Day, and the Epilogue

**The Third Day**

The morning of the third day was clear and fresh. Again the men went up to the mast-heads, and again Ahab sat in his basket at the top of the main mast. But for many hours nobody saw the whale.

"Have we passed him?" Ahab said. "Yes, he's following us now, and we are not following him. I've gone the wrong way." He gave orders to turn the ship round. Now we were sailing against the wind, back the way we had come.

"Against the wind he now steers for the open jaw," whispered Starbuck to himself. "May God be with us all."

While he was on the mast, Ahab looked at the sea and the sky and spoke to himself. "What a lovely day again! If the world were new, made as a summer house for the angels, the day could not be lovelier. There is a lot here to think about, if Ahab had time to think. But Ahab never thinks; he only feels, feels, feels. That's enough for any man!"

At noon, the lookouts shouted. There was the spout, not far away.

"Forehead to forehead I meet you, this third time, Moby Dick!" cried Ahab. "Down, men! Lower the boats!"

Before he got into his boat, Ahab turned to Starbuck.

"Starbuck!"

"Sir?"

"For the third time my soul's ship begins this voyage, Starbuck."

"Yes, sir. You want it so."

"Some ships sail from their ports and are never seen again, Starbuck."

"That is true, sir. Very sadly true."

"Some men die when the tide goes out; some when the sea is low; some when it is high. And I feel now like a wave at its highest point, Starbuck, just before it breaks. I am old. Shake hands with me, man."

Their hands met. Their eyes met. Starbuck's tears held them together like glue.

"Oh, my captain, my captain! Noble heart — don't go! Don't go! Look, a brave man is crying. It hurts me so much to beg you!"

"Lower away!" cried Ahab, pulling his hand away. "Stand by the crew!"

The boats were lowered, and they rowed out towards the whale. As they went, Starbuck stood at the side of the ship and watched them. And he saw that Ahab's boat was followed by sharks. They swam close to it, biting at the oars as the men rowed. They followed only Ahab's boat. "Sharks!" Starbuck said to himself. "They follow him the way vultures follow armies. Oh, my God! Why do I feel so terribly calm, when I am expecting the worst?"

**The First Hearse**

The whale came up suddenly in front of the boats, rising out of the sea, huge and white, with water pouring from his sides. And then the men saw something horrible.

Tied to the whale's back by the tangled lines from the day before was the body of Fedallah. His clothes were torn, and his dark, open eyes looked straight at Ahab.

Ahab dropped his harpoon. "Tricked! Tricked!" he cried, breathing hard. "Yes, Parsee! I see you again. Yes, you go before me. And this, then, is the hearse that you promised — a hearse not made by human hands." The whale was the first hearse.

But Moby Dick did not attack. He seemed to want only to escape. He began to swim away, steadily, towards the open sea.

"Oh, Ahab," cried Starbuck from the ship, "even now, on the third day, it is not too late to stop. See! Moby Dick does not look for you. It is you, you, that madly look for him!"

But Ahab did not hear. His boat followed the whale.

**The Second Hearse**

Then the whale turned. Perhaps he was tired of being chased. Perhaps he understood at last that the ship was the source of all his enemies. He turned round and swam straight towards the *Pequod*, with his great white forehead in front of him, through the foaming sea.

"The whale! The whale! Turn the ship! Turn the ship!" cried Starbuck. "Oh, sweet powers of heaven, hold me close! If Starbuck must die, let him die bravely. My God, I hold on to You. Must I die like this, facing my end?"

Stubb, too, stood on the deck. "Oh, Flask," he said, "I'd give anything for one red cherry before we die! I wish we were where cherries grow. Oh, Flask, I hope my poor mother has already received some of my pay. If not, she won't get much now, because the voyage is over."

The whale hit the ship with his great head. The *Pequod* shook from end to end. The men fell to the deck. A great hole was broken in her side below the water, and the sea rushed in. The whale went under the ship and came up on the other side, close to Ahab's boat.

Ahab saw the *Pequod* sinking.

"The ship! The hearse!" he cried. "The second hearse! And its wood could only be American!" He understood the prophecy at last.

"Oh, a lonely death after a lonely life!" he cried. "Now I feel that my greatest greatness is in my greatest sorrow. Come, all you waves of my past life, and break over me in this last great wave of my death! Towards you I go, you whale that destroys everything but is never defeated. I will fight you to the end. From the heart of hell I stab at you. For hate's sake I spit my last breath at you. Let all coffins and all hearses sink together! And since neither can be mine, let me be torn to pieces while I still chase you, tied to you, you damned whale! So — I throw the spear!"

He threw the harpoon with all his strength. It went deep into the whale. The whale rushed forward, and the line ran out at great speed. It got tangled, and Ahab bent down to free it. He freed it — but the line flew round his neck like a rope round the neck of a man being hanged, and pulled him out of the boat so fast that his men did not even see him go. He disappeared into the sea. The hemp had killed him, as Fedallah had said.

For a moment, the men in the boat stood frozen. Then they turned and looked at the ship. "The ship? Great God, where is the ship?"

The *Pequod* was sinking. Only the tops of her masts were still above the water. On the top of the main mast, Tashtego was still there, holding a hammer, nailing Ahab's red flag to the mast. As the ship went down, a great whirlpool formed, turning round and round. It caught the last boat and the men in it, and pulled them down into the sea. Everything — the men, the boats, the broken oars — went round and round, and disappeared.

At the very last moment, a sea-hawk flew down to the top of the mast and caught at the flag with its beak. Just then, Tashtego's hammer came down and nailed the bird's wing to the wood. And so the bird of heaven, screaming, went down with Ahab's ship, like a devil that would not go to hell without dragging a living part of heaven with it.

Now small birds flew screaming over the open hole in the water. A white wave beat against its steep sides. Then the whirlpool closed, and the great shroud of the sea rolled on as it rolled five thousand years ago.

**Epilogue**

The drama is over. Why, then, does anyone come forward to tell the story? Because one man survived.

On the third day, Fedallah's place in Ahab's boat had to be filled, and I was the one who took it. When the boat was hit and turned over, I was thrown out, and I floated in the sea at a distance, watching everything happen. When I reached the place where the ship had sunk, the whirlpool had become smaller. I was pulled slowly towards its centre, round and round, like a man on a slowly turning wheel.

When I reached the centre, the black bubble in the middle burst. And suddenly, from the depths, Queequeg's coffin shot up out of the water, like a living thing, and fell beside me. It was the life-buoy, made of Queequeg's coffin. I held onto it, and it saved my life.

For almost a whole day and a whole night, I floated on that soft and funeral-like sea, holding the coffin. The sharks swam past me without touching me, as if their mouths were locked. The wild sea-hawks flew over me with their beaks closed.

On the second day, a ship came near, and at last it picked me up. It was the *Rachel*. She was still sailing around, searching for her missing children. But she only found another orphan.
