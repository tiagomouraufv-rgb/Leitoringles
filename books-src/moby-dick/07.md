## Part 7 — Captain Ahab

For several days after leaving Nantucket, nobody saw Captain Ahab. The three mates took turns on watch, and they gave all the orders. Sometimes they went down into the captain's cabin and came back up with orders from him, as if they were taking messages from a hidden god. I began to feel uneasy. I remembered Elijah's strange words.

The ship sailed south. The weather was still cold and grey, and the sea was rough. Then one morning, as I went up on deck for the morning watch, I looked towards the back of the ship, the part called the **quarter-deck**, and a shiver ran through me. Captain Ahab was standing there.

He looked like a man who has been burned alive and has not been destroyed — like a statue made of bronze. He was tall and wide, and he stood very straight. A long, thin scar ran down from his grey hair, across one side of his face and neck, and disappeared into his clothes. It looked like the mark that lightning leaves on a great tree, when it runs down the trunk without breaking it. Nobody knew if the scar went all the way down his body. An old sailor said that Ahab had been born with it. Others said he had got it in a fight at sea.

But the strangest thing about him was his leg. His real leg had been taken by a whale. Now he stood on an artificial leg made of white ivory — polished bone from the jaw of a sperm whale. On each side of the quarter-deck, there was a small hole in the wooden floor, about half an inch deep. Ahab put the end of his ivory leg into one of these holes. That kept him steady when the ship rolled. He stood there, looking straight ahead over the front of the ship, with a terrible, determined look in his eyes. He did not say a word. Nobody spoke to him. But you could see that he was suffering from some great, secret pain.

After that first morning, he came up on deck almost every day. Sometimes he stood in his hole. Sometimes he sat on a stool made of ivory. Sometimes he walked up and down the deck for hours. As the weather got warmer and we sailed into the south, his face seemed to get a little softer, like an old tree in spring. But the sad, hard look never left him for long.

**Ahab and Stubb**

Ahab did not sleep much. Old men often sleep very little, and Ahab slept less than any man I ever knew. At night, he often walked on the deck for hours, while the men tried to sleep below. Every step of his ivory leg on the wooden floor sounded like a hammer knocking on a coffin, and the sailors in their beds could hear it.

One night, Stubb went up to him and politely asked him if he could put something soft on the end of his ivory leg, to make it quieter.

Ahab turned round and looked at him. "Am I a cannon-ball, Stubb," he said, "that you want to wrap me in cloth? Go away. I had forgotten. Go down to your bed — your nightly grave, where men like you sleep between sheets, to get used to the coffin that will hold you one day. Down, dog, to your kennel!"

Stubb was shocked. "I am not used to being spoken to like that, sir," he said. "I don't like it at all."

"Go! Or I'll remove you from this world!" cried Ahab, and he came towards Stubb with such a terrible face that Stubb stepped back.

"Nobody ever spoke to me like that without getting a hard punch," said Stubb to himself as he went down the stairs. "It's very strange. I don't know whether to go back and hit him, or to kneel down here and pray for him. Yes, that was the thought that came into my head. But I have never prayed in my life! He's the strangest old man I ever sailed with. How his eyes burned at me! Is he mad?"

That night Stubb had a strange dream. He dreamed that Ahab kicked him with his ivory leg. When Stubb tried to kick back, his leg came off. Then a little old man with a hump on his back appeared and said, "Why do you complain? Being kicked by a great man is an honour. Old Ahab is a great man. You should be proud that he kicked you." When Stubb woke up, he told the dream to Flask. "Flask," he said, "I think the best thing is to let that old man alone. Never speak to him, whatever he says."

**The Pipe**

When Stubb had gone, Ahab sat down on his ivory stool and asked for his pipe. He lit it and began to smoke. But after a while, he took it out of his mouth.

"What is this?" he said to himself. "Smoking no longer calms me. Oh, my pipe! Why do I need you? A pipe is for peaceful men, for calm thoughts and soft white hair — not for torn grey hair like mine. I'll smoke no more."

And he threw the pipe, still burning, into the sea. The fire hissed in the waves. The ship sailed on, leaving it behind. Then Ahab walked away, with his hat pulled down over his eyes.

From that night, everyone on the ship felt that something was wrong. The captain was a man with a terrible secret, and sooner or later we would all know what it was.
