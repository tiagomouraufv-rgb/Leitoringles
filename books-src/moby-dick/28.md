## Part 28 — The Prophecy

**The Dying Whale**

A day after the *Bachelor*, we found a group of whales, and we killed four of them. One of them was killed by Ahab's boat. It was late in the afternoon. The sun was setting over the sea in red and gold.

As the whale died, a strange thing happened. Slowly, the great head turned towards the sun, as if the dying animal wanted to look at it one last time. Then it stopped moving. Ahab watched in silence.

"He turns and turns him to the sun," Ahab said quietly. "How slowly, but how surely! He, too, worships fire. Most faithful servant of the sun! But see — no sooner dead than death turns him round, and he looks another way. Oh, dark half of the world, where you worship fire, I believe you, but I do not trust you. Here, in the sea, the waves carry the dead, and the water has no god."

**The Night Watch**

That night, Ahab's boat stayed beside the dead whale, far from the ship, to keep it safe until morning. Ahab sat in the back of the boat, and Fedallah sat in the front. The other men were asleep. In the darkness, the two of them talked.

"I have dreamed it again," said Ahab.

"Of the hearses?" asked Fedallah. A **hearse** is the carriage that carries a dead body to the grave. "Have I not said, old man, that neither hearse nor coffin can be yours?"

"And who are buried at sea, who have no hearses?"

"But I said, old man, that before you can die on this voyage, two hearses must be seen by you on the sea. The first is not made by mortal hands. And the second must be made of wood that grew in America."

"Ha, ha!" said Ahab. "A strange sight that would be, Fedallah — a hearse with its plumes floating on the ocean, with the waves as pallbearers. We won't see such a sight soon."

"Believe it or not, you cannot die till it is seen, old man."

"And what was it you said about yourself?"

"Though it comes to the last, I shall still go before you, as your pilot."

"And when you go before me — if that ever happens — then, before I can follow you, you must still appear to me, to guide me? Was it not so? Well, I believe it all, my pilot. And I have here two promises. I will kill Moby Dick and survive."

"Take another promise, old man," said Fedallah, his eyes shining like fireflies in the dark. "Only **hemp** can kill you."

"The gallows, you mean? A hanging rope?" laughed Ahab. "Then I am immortal, on land and on sea!" And both were silent again, while the dawn came up grey over the sea.

**The Quadrant**

The season-on-the-line was coming at last — the time when Moby Dick was usually seen near the equator. Every day at noon, Ahab sat on the deck with his **quadrant**, a navigation instrument, and measured the height of the sun to find out exactly where the ship was.

One day, after he had made his measurement, he looked at the quadrant with anger.

"You toy!" he said. "You foolish plaything of proud men! You can tell me where I am, and where the sun is. But you cannot tell me where Moby Dick is! You cannot tell me where I shall be tomorrow at noon. Science! Curse you, you useless toy! Curse all things that lead men's eyes up to the heavens, when they should be looking at the earth! I will no longer use you to guide my ship!"

And he threw the quadrant down onto the deck and broke it with his feet — with his living foot and his ivory foot.

"From now on, the compass and the log and line will guide me," he said. "I'll find my way across the sea as the old sailors did."

Fedallah, who was standing nearby, smiled, as if he knew more than Ahab. Starbuck and Stubb, at the other side of the deck, saw everything.

"I have sat before the hot coals," whispered Starbuck to himself, "and have seen them burn down to dead ashes. Old man, your life will burn down to ashes too."

"Yes," said Stubb quietly. "Smashed it, did he? Well, it doesn't matter. Whatever happens, I'll keep on laughing. Wise Stubb — that's what they call me."
