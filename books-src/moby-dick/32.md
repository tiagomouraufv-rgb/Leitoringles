## Part 32 — The Chase: The First and Second Days

**The First Day**

That night, at about midnight, Ahab suddenly stood up on the deck and smelled the air, like a dog. He said that a whale must be near. He could smell the strange smell that a living sperm whale gives out over long distances. He changed the ship's course a little and ordered the sails to be reduced.

At dawn, all the men were at the mast-heads, looking at the sea. Ahab was pulled up in his basket. The sea was smooth and quiet. Then, at the same moment, all the lookouts saw it — and Ahab saw it first.

"There she blows! There she blows! A hump like a snow-hill! It is Moby Dick!"

The cry went through the whole ship. The men ran up the ropes to see. About a mile ahead, a huge white hump was moving slowly through the blue sea, sending up a tall, white spout every few seconds.

"And did none of you see it before?" cried Ahab.

"I saw him almost at the same moment, sir, as Captain Ahab did, and I cried out," said Tashtego.

"Not the same moment. Not the same — no, the doubloon is mine. Fate saved the doubloon for me. I only. None of you could have raised the White Whale first. There she blows! Lower the boats! Mr Starbuck, remember — stay on board and keep the ship. Lower!"

Ahab's boat had been repaired. It went first, with Fedallah and his dark men rowing. Stubb's and Flask's boats followed. Starbuck stayed on the ship.

As we came close, the white whale was swimming quietly, almost peacefully. The sea around him was smooth and calm. He was more beautiful than I had ever imagined — calm and gentle and grand, with a soft, white, milky body. Seabirds flew above him. In his back, the old broken harpoons stood up like the masts of sunken ships. No god, not even Jupiter in the form of a white bull swimming with a beautiful girl on his back, ever looked so majestic.

Then, slowly, he raised his great tail into the air and went down under the water.

The boats waited. A long time passed. The birds flew in circles. Then Ahab looked down into the clear water, and he saw a small white spot, rising quickly from the depths. It grew bigger and bigger. Then he saw two long, crooked rows of white teeth. It was Moby Dick's open mouth, coming straight up from below, under Ahab's boat!

Ahab turned the boat quickly. But the whale was too fast. He came up beside the boat and took the front of it in his huge jaws, the way a cat takes a mouse. His crooked lower jaw went under the boat, and his teeth came down on the wood. Ahab grabbed the jaw with his bare hands and tried to push it away. Then the jaw closed, and the boat was bitten completely in half. Ahab fell into the sea.

The whale swam round and round the broken pieces of the boat, making waves. The men in the water held onto the floating wood. Ahab, who was too old and tired to swim well, held onto a piece of the boat and shouted, "Sail on the whale! Drive him off!"

The *Pequod* came forward, straight towards the whale. Moby Dick swam slowly away. The other boats came and picked up Ahab and his men. Ahab lay at the bottom of Stubb's boat, exhausted, like a man trampled by elephants. For a long time he could not speak. Then he sat up.

"The harpoon?" he said. "Is it safe?"

"Yes, sir. It wasn't thrown. Here it is."

"Put it in front of me. Any men missing?"

"One, two, three, four, five — there were five oars, sir, and here are five men."

"That's good. Help me up, man. I want to stand. There, there! I see him! There! Still going away from the wind. What a spout! Let go of me! New life is running through Ahab's bones again! Set the sail! Oars out! To the helm!"

They followed the whale all day, but he swam too fast. As the night came, Ahab gave orders to follow him through the darkness, and to call him at the first sign of the whale.

"The doubloon is mine," he said to the men, "but I will leave it on the mast. Whoever sees the whale first on the day he is killed shall have it. And if he is killed on that day, then ten times its value shall be divided among you all."

**The Second Day**

At dawn, the lookouts saw him again. This time, Moby Dick jumped right out of the water, like a salmon jumping up a waterfall — the whole huge body, high into the air! Then he fell back with a crash that sent the white water flying high, like a mountain of snow.

"Yes, jump for the last time in the sun, Moby Dick!" cried Ahab. "Your hour and your harpoon are near! Down, down, all of you! Lower! Mr Starbuck, stay on board!"

Ahab had another boat now, a spare one. All three boats were lowered. This time, Moby Dick did not wait for them. He turned and came straight towards the boats, with his great head forward, his jaw open, and his tail beating the sea behind him.

The three boats spread out to attack him from three sides. The harpoons flew, one after another, and three of them hit him. But the whale swam round and round among the boats, so fast that the lines from the three harpoons crossed and tangled together. Ahab cut his line to free it, but it was too late. The whale pulled the lines tighter and tighter, and the boats of Stubb and Flask were pulled together and smashed against each other, like two shells on a beach. The men fell into the water.

Then the whale went down and came up right under Ahab's boat, and threw it high into the air. It turned over and fell back upside down into the sea. Ahab and his men were thrown into the water.

The *Pequod* came and picked up all the men. But Ahab was hurt. His ivory leg had broken off, and only a short, sharp piece of it was left. He leaned on Starbuck's shoulder.

"Yes, Starbuck," he said. "It is sweet to lean on someone sometimes, whoever he is. I wish old Ahab had leaned on others more often."

"Captain, the leg is broken," said the carpenter. "There's only a stump left."

"Even with a broken leg, Ahab is not hurt," said Ahab. "No white whale, no man and no devil can touch the real Ahab, deep inside. Count the men!"

The men were counted. One was missing.

"The Parsee!" cried Stubb. "Fedallah! He's gone."

They searched everywhere on the ship, but Fedallah was nowhere. Then Stubb remembered that he had seen him caught in the tangled lines, when Ahab's boat turned over. The whale must have pulled him down into the sea.

"Caught in the line!" cried Ahab. "My line! Gone! Gone! What does that little word mean? It sounds like a funeral bell, and old Ahab shakes like a bell tower! Fedallah gone first!" He remembered the prophecy: Fedallah would go before him, as his pilot.

"Captain Ahab," said Starbuck, "you will never, never catch him, old man. In the name of Jesus, stop this. It is worse than the devil's madness. Two days of chasing, two boats smashed to pieces. Your own leg broken again. Your dark shadow, Fedallah, gone. All the good angels have been warning you. What more do you want? Shall we follow this murderous fish until he kills every last one of us? Shall he pull us down to the bottom of the sea, or to hell? It is a sin to hunt him any more!"

"Starbuck," said Ahab, "lately I have felt strangely close to you, since the day we both saw — you know what — in each other's eyes. But in this matter of the whale, your face must be nothing to me, like an empty hand. Ahab is for ever Ahab, man. Everything that happens here was decided long ago, and it cannot be changed. You and I practised these parts a billion years before this ocean existed. Fool! I am only Fate's officer. I am following orders. And you, who are below me, must follow mine. Stand around me, men. You see an old man with only a stump of a leg, leaning on a broken lance. That is Ahab's body. But Ahab's soul is like a centipede: it walks on a hundred legs.

"Do you believe in signs? Then laugh! Before a drowning man drowns, he comes up to the surface twice. Then he comes up once more, and sinks for ever. It is the same with Moby Dick. For two days he has come up. Tomorrow will be the third day. Yes, men, he will come up once more — but only to spout for the last time! Do you feel brave, men?"

"As fearless fire," cried Stubb.

"And as mechanical," Ahab whispered to himself. Then he went to his cabin, while the carpenter made him a new leg from the white bone of the broken boat.
