## Part 10 — Night Thoughts

**Sunset**

That evening, Ahab sat alone in his cabin, looking out of the window at the sea behind the ship. The sun was going down, and the water was red and gold.

"I leave a white and troubled wake," he said to himself. "Wherever I sail, the water turns pale behind me. Envious waves rise up to cover my track, but I do not care.

"The sun is going down. It is setting in the sea, as it does every evening. And it goes down so peacefully! But I — I cannot enjoy any beautiful thing. I have the power to understand beauty, but not the power to feel it. Damned in the middle of paradise! Good night, good night.

"It was easy to make the men follow me. They were like a heap of gunpowder, and I was the match. Only Starbuck was difficult. But even he will follow me now, because he is too weak to fight me.

"They think I am mad. Starbuck thinks so. But I am more than mad. I am madness made mad! I know exactly what I am doing. My fixed purpose runs on iron rails, and nothing can stop it. Over the deep valleys, through the hearts of mountains, under the rivers — I rush straight ahead! Nothing stands in my way."

**Dusk**

At the same time, Starbuck was standing by the main mast, thinking.

"My soul is defeated," he said to himself. "I am defeated by a madman! He told the crew his plan, and I could do nothing. I saw his purpose in his eyes, and I felt that I must help him, though I hate it. He will drive us all to death, and I must go with him. What a terrible old man! He wants no one above him, but he rules everyone below him like a king!

"Oh, I see my bad end. But I will help him. I must. I cannot fight him, because he is the captain, and because his will is stronger than mine. But oh, God! I wish I could. I think of my wife and my child, far away in Nantucket. Will I ever see them again? Oh, life! I feel the danger now. I am like a man in a dark forest, and wild animals are all around me. Help me, God, to stay strong!"

From the front of the ship came the sound of the sailors singing and laughing.

**The First Night Watch**

Stubb was alone on deck, smoking his pipe and thinking.

"Ha! ha! ha! ha! hem! Clear my throat! I've been thinking about it ever since, and the result is: ha, ha! Why? Because a laugh is the wisest, easiest answer to all that's strange. Whatever happens, there's always one comfort — everything is fixed by fate. I heard everything that the old man said to Starbuck. Starbuck felt the same as I did the other night, when the old man spoke to me. Well, I'll laugh. Life is a joke. Whatever comes, I'll go to it laughing."

**Midnight**

Down in the front of the ship, in the forecastle, the sailors had a party. They had drunk the captain's drink, and they were happy and excited. They sang and danced.

A sailor from Nantucket sang a song about whaling. A sailor from the Isle of Man said that the gold coin would bring bad luck. A Dutch sailor slept and woke up and slept again. A French sailor wanted to dance, and he called for Pip and his tambourine. Pip played, and men from Holland, Iceland, Malta, Sicily, the Azores, China and Tahiti all danced together. Some danced so wildly that they nearly broke their legs.

A young Tahitian sailor lay on a mat and dreamed of the green hills and the dark-eyed girls of his island. An old sailor from the Isle of Man looked at the sky and said that a storm was coming.

Then a Spanish sailor began to make jokes about Daggoo's black skin. Daggoo became angry and jumped up, ready to fight. "Who's afraid of black?" he cried. In a moment, all the sailors were standing in a circle around them, shouting, "A ring! A ring!"

But just then, the storm came. Lightning flashed across the sky, and thunder roared. "Hands by the halyards! In topgallant sails!" shouted the mate. The fight was forgotten, and everyone ran to take in the sails before the wind could tear them. Only little Pip was left behind. He crawled under the windlass, frightened, with his tambourine in his hands.

"Oh, thunder! Oh, lightning!" he cried. "Jimmini, what a squall! Oh, I wish I was home in Alabama. White squalls, white whales! I heard them talking. The old man made them swear to hunt the white whale. Oh, big white God up there somewhere in the darkness, have mercy on this small black boy down here! Save him from all the men who have no hearts to be afraid!"
