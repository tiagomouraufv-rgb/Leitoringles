## Part 12 — The First Lowering

**Strange Sounds**

One night, a sailor named Archy, who was passing buckets of water along a line of men, whispered to the man next to him, "Did you hear that noise? There's somebody down in the hold. I heard a cough." The other man laughed at him. But Archy was sure. "There's somebody down there that we haven't seen on deck," he said. "I think the old man knows about it. Remember the dark figures that came on board in Nantucket, before we sailed?"

**The Charts**

At night, Ahab often sat alone in his cabin with big old maps — **charts** of the oceans — spread out on the table in front of him. He studied them for hours. With a pencil, he drew lines across the empty blue spaces.

What was he doing? He was trying to find Moby Dick. Sperm whales, like birds, travel along fixed routes at different times of the year. They follow their food. Whalemen know these routes, and they know which seas are good for whaling in which season. Ahab had collected all the information he could find from the logbooks of many ships. He knew where Moby Dick had been seen, and when. And he knew that every year, in a certain season, the white whale was likely to be in a certain part of the Pacific Ocean, near the equator. That was where Ahab wanted to meet him. But it was still a year away. Until then, the *Pequod* would hunt whales in other seas, as on any normal voyage.

The thought of Moby Dick never left Ahab, even in his sleep. Sometimes, in the middle of the night, he woke up screaming and ran out of his cabin, as if his bed were on fire. He was tortured by his own thoughts. His mad purpose had become a living thing inside him, eating him from the inside like a vulture.

**Whales That Fight Back**

Some people on land may think that the story of Moby Dick is impossible — that no whale could be so clever and so dangerous. But it is true that whales sometimes fight back. I know of many cases. Some whales have been chased by different ships for years, and have become famous among whalemen, with names like Timor Tom or New Zealand Jack.

And whales have even sunk ships. In 1820, a large sperm whale attacked the whaling ship *Essex* in the Pacific. It hit the ship twice with its huge head, and broke a hole in her side. The ship sank. Her crew escaped in small boats and suffered terribly for many weeks before a few of them were rescued. So the story of the white whale is not impossible at all.

**Ahab's Thoughts**

Ahab knew that his crew would not stay excited about the white whale for ever. Sailors are simple men, he thought. They need money and they need action. If months passed without any whales, they might turn against him. Starbuck, too, might try to take the ship away from him. So Ahab decided to let the *Pequod* hunt whales normally until they found Moby Dick. That would keep the men busy and happy.

**The Mat-Maker**

It was a cloudy, quiet afternoon. Queequeg and I were weaving a mat on the deck. I passed a thread backwards and forwards between the long threads, and Queequeg pushed it down with a piece of wood. It seemed to me like the loom of Time. The fixed threads were necessity; my hand moving the thread was free will; and Queequeg's piece of wood, hitting the threads in different ways, was chance.

Suddenly, a wild cry came from above. It was Tashtego, at the mast-head.

"There she blows! There! There! There! She blows! She blows!"

"Where-away?"

"On the lee-beam, about two miles off! A school of them!"

Immediately, everyone on the ship was moving. The sperm whales were swimming along the surface, about two miles away, blowing up spouts of water into the air.

"Lower the boats!" Ahab shouted. "Clear away the boats!"

And then something happened that nobody expected. Five dark figures appeared on deck, as if they had come out of the air. They were Ahab's secret crew — the men we had heard in the hold! Four of them had yellow skin and wore black Chinese jackets. The fifth was a tall, dark man with a white turban on his head and one white tooth sticking out from his lips. His name was Fedallah. Nobody had ever seen him before. They went straight to a spare boat, which everyone had thought was only for emergencies, and they got it ready. It was Ahab's boat. He would hunt whales himself, with his own private crew.

The sailors stared at them in amazement. But there was no time to ask questions. "Lower away, men!" cried the mates.

**The Chase**

We rowed fast towards the whales. I was in Starbuck's boat, with Queequeg as harpooner. Stubb, with his pipe in his mouth, encouraged his men in a calm, friendly voice. "Pull, my children! Pull, my little ones! Why don't you pull? Pull your backs out! Easy, easy! Don't be in a hurry. But pull, damn you, pull!" Flask stood on the back of his boat, jumping up and down with excitement.

Ahab's boat was the fastest. His dark men rowed like machines.

The sky grew darker, and the wind began to rise. A storm was coming. The whales went down under the water and came up again further away. Starbuck's boat was separated from the others.

"There's white water ahead!" whispered Starbuck. "Get ready, Queequeg!"

Queequeg stood up in the front of the boat with his harpoon. Just as a huge whale rose out of the water in front of us, he threw it. At the same moment, the storm hit us. The boat was thrown into the air. The whale touched it and swam away, and the harpoon slid out of its back. We were all thrown into the sea. When we came up, the boat was full of water, and the wind and the waves were all around us.

We climbed back into the half-sunk boat. The other boats were gone. The ship was gone. It was getting dark, and the storm was getting worse. We were alone in the middle of the ocean.

Starbuck lit a lantern and gave it to Queequeg, who held it up on a pole, as a sign of hope in the darkness. All night long we sat in the water-filled boat, wet and cold, waiting. Queequeg held up the lantern the whole night, like a man without hope holding up hope in the middle of despair.

At dawn, through the fog, we suddenly saw the *Pequod*. She was coming straight towards us! She was going to run over us! We jumped into the sea just as the great ship hit our boat. We were pulled on board, half dead, but alive.

**My Will**

When I was warm and dry again, I asked Stubb if this sort of thing happened often in whaling.

"Very often," he said. "It's nothing."

"And is it normal," I asked Flask, "for the mates to send their boats into a storm, in the dark, when they can't see anything?"

"Of course," said Flask. "That's whaling."

So I decided that if death was so close in this business, I should be ready for it. I went down to my bed and wrote my will. Queequeg was my witness. When I had finished, I felt calm and happy, as if a stone had rolled away from my heart. Now I could laugh at death. I had already died once, in a way; everything after this was extra life, a gift.

**Fedallah**

After that day, all the sailors talked about Fedallah and his men. Who were they? Where had they come from? How had Ahab hidden them on the ship? Some sailors said that Fedallah was the devil himself, and that Ahab had sold his soul to him. Stubb said it seriously and laughed at the same time.

Ahab's officers were especially surprised. A whaling captain usually does not lower his own boat. It is too dangerous for him, because the ship needs her captain. And Ahab had only one leg! Peleg and Bildad would never have allowed it. That was why he had hidden his men.

But Fedallah stayed mysterious. He hardly ever spoke. He was always near Ahab, watching him, like his shadow. At night he stood on deck looking at the sea. He seemed to be part of Ahab's dark secret.
