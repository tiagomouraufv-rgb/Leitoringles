## Part 29 — The Candles and the Musket

**The Typhoon**

In the warm seas near Japan, the most terrible storms in the world can appear suddenly, out of a clear blue sky. These storms are called **typhoons**. One evening, a typhoon hit the *Pequod*.

The wind tore her sails to pieces. The waves were like mountains, and they broke over the deck. One of the whaleboats — Ahab's own boat — was smashed by a wave. The thunder was so loud that we could not hear each other's voices. The rain fell in sheets. It was as dark as midnight.

Then, suddenly, a strange light appeared. At the top of each mast, and at the end of every pole, a pale white fire was burning, like the flame of a tall candle. The three masts looked like three giant candles in front of an altar. Sailors call this strange light **corposants**, or Saint Elmo's fire. They believe it is a sign — sometimes of good luck, but more often of danger.

Everyone stopped working and stared at the white flames. Some sailors fell on their knees. Their faces looked pale and ghostly in the white light. Stubb shouted, "The corposants have mercy on us all!" Even the harpooners were afraid.

Ahab came forward. He stood under the main mast, with his foot on the back of Fedallah, who knelt in front of him. He held up his hand to the white fire on the top of the mast, and he spoke to it in a loud voice.

"Oh, you clear spirit of clear fire! I worshipped you once, on these seas, as a Persian worshipper of fire, until you burned me, and I still have the scar. Now I know you better, and I know that the right way to worship you is to defy you! You are a great power, but you have no love and no pity. I am a man, and I have a personality, and a will of my own. You can kill me, but you cannot make me obey you. Oh, you clear spirit, of your fire you made me, and like a true child of fire, I breathe it back to you!"

At that moment, the harpoon that Ahab had forged with the blood of the harpooners — which was lying in his broken boat — began to burn with a white flame too, like a giant tongue of fire.

Starbuck took Ahab by the arm. "God, God is against you, old man! Stop! It is a bad sign. Let me take the ship home, as she should go. We have had a bad voyage, and it is going to be worse. Let us go home, while we can still save the ship and our lives!"

The sailors heard Starbuck and began to shout. They ran to the ropes, ready to turn the ship and run before the wind towards home. But Ahab grabbed the burning harpoon and held it up like a torch.

"All your oaths to hunt the White Whale are as binding as mine!" he cried. "And heart, soul and body — Ahab is bound. Look! This is how I blow out the last fear!" With one breath, he blew out the flame on the harpoon.

The sailors ran away from him in terror. Nobody spoke against him again.

**Later That Night**

Later, while the storm was still blowing, Stubb and Flask stood together in the darkness.

"Did you hear what Starbuck said?" asked Flask. "That God is against us?"

"Of course God is against us," said Stubb. "But that doesn't mean we will die. The ship is safe enough. Ahab put his lightning rod over the side, so the lightning will go into the sea. Don't worry, Flask. A storm is a storm."

High up on the mast, Tashtego was tying the sail. "Um, um, um," he sang to himself. "Stop that thunder! Plenty too much thunder up here. What's the use of thunder? Um, um, um. We don't want thunder; we want rum."

**The Musket**

At about midnight, the typhoon became weaker. Then the wind changed direction. It was now a good wind, blowing from behind, pushing the *Pequod* towards the place where Ahab wanted to go. Starbuck went down to the captain's cabin to tell him.

Ahab was asleep in his cabin. As Starbuck stood in front of the door, he saw the row of loaded muskets on the wall. One of them was the same musket that Ahab had pointed at him a few weeks before.

Starbuck took it down. A terrible idea came into his head.

"He would have killed me once," he thought. "He is going to kill us all. He is leading the whole crew to death. And he won't listen to anyone. Is there no other way? Could I lock him up and take the ship home? No — he is too strong. Even with chains, he would be more dangerous than a tiger in a cage. I could not bear his howls. The only way is to kill him. If I shoot him now, while he sleeps, the ship will be saved, and the crew too. And I will see my wife and my child again. Oh, Mary! Mary! Boy! Boy! Boy! But if I do not do it, old man, who knows where you will take us? Great God, where are You? Shall I? Shall I?"

He raised the musket and pointed it at the door. His hand was shaking. At that moment, Ahab cried out in his sleep, on the other side of the door:

"Stern all! Oh, Moby Dick, I hold your heart at last!"

Starbuck stood still, as if he had been struck. Then, with a shaking hand, he put the musket back on the wall.

He went back up to the deck and said to Stubb: "He's too sound asleep, Mr Stubb. Go down and wake him, and tell him about the wind. I must watch the deck. You know what to say."
