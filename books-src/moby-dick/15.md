## Part 15 — Pictures of Whales and the Giant Squid

**Bad Pictures**

If you want to know what a whale really looks like, do not trust the pictures in books. Most of them are completely wrong. Old painters drew whales like monsters from fairy tales, with heads like dragons, or bodies like huge fish with scales. Even in modern scientific books, the pictures are often bad. Why? Because the artists have never seen a living whale. A whale on a beach, dead and broken, does not look like a whale in the sea. And a living whale shows only a small part of its body above the water. So the only way to know what a whale looks like is to go whaling yourself. But that is dangerous. You may be broken to pieces by the whale before you have a chance to look at him properly.

There are a few good pictures, though, made by French artists, which show whaling scenes with real energy and truth: the boats, the harpooners, the great whale rising out of the sea.

**Whales in Bone, Wood and Stars**

Sailors on whaling ships also make their own pictures. In their free time, they carve whales and whaling scenes on whale teeth and bones, using only their knives. This art is called **scrimshaw**. Some of it is very fine and delicate.

And if you look carefully, you can see the shape of whales everywhere: in the clouds, in the mountains, even in the stars at night. There are groups of stars in the sky that look like a whale. I have seen them, sitting at the mast-head, on beautiful clear nights.

**Brit**

As we sailed north-east from the island of Crozet, we came into a part of the ocean where the water looked like a yellow field of wheat. This yellow stuff is called **brit**. It is made of millions of tiny sea creatures. Right whales feed on it. They swim slowly through the yellow water with their mouths open, and the brit stays in their long, thin whalebone, like a sieve.

We saw many right whales feeding there, swimming slowly, like mowers cutting grass in a field. But we did not hunt them, because the *Pequod* hunted only sperm whales.

Looking at the sea, I thought how terrible and how beautiful it is. On land, everything is familiar and safe. But the sea is cruel. Its creatures eat each other, and have done so since the world began. And yet, in the middle of all that danger, there is a green, peaceful island in every man's soul. Do not leave it, or you may never come back.

**The Giant Squid**

One quiet, sunny morning, Daggoo, at the main mast-head, saw something strange in the sea, far away. It was a great white shape, rising slowly from the deep, shining like snow. Then it sank again. Then it rose once more.

"There! There again!" cried Daggoo. "The White Whale, the White Whale!"

Immediately the boats were lowered. Ahab stood in the front of his boat, and all the men rowed hard towards the white shape. But when we came close, it was not Moby Dick.

It was the strangest thing a man could see in the sea: an enormous, soft, white mass, long and wide, floating on the water. From its centre came a great number of long arms, curling and twisting like snakes, as if they wanted to catch anything near them. It had no face. It looked like nothing living. It was a **giant squid**.

As we watched, it slowly sank again and disappeared.

"I would rather have seen Moby Dick and fought him," said Starbuck, very pale, "than have seen that white ghost!"

Ahab said nothing. He turned his boat and went back to the ship.

Very few whalemen have ever seen the giant squid, and those who see it usually go home and talk about it for the rest of their lives. Some believe it is the largest living creature in the ocean. But Queequeg had a different idea. "When you see him squid," he said, "then you quick see him sperm whale." Sperm whales eat squid, he explained, so where there are squid, there are sperm whales.
