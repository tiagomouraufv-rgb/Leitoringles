## Part 17 — Sharks, the Head and the Jeroboam

**The Sharks**

That night, there were so many sharks around the dead whale that the mates feared they would eat it all before the morning. So Queequeg and another sailor stood on a platform over the side of the ship, with lanterns, and killed the sharks with long, sharp **spades**, cutting into their heads. But the more sharks they killed, the more came. The sharks were so mad with hunger that they attacked each other. They bit their own wounds and ate their own insides.

When Queequeg pulled a dead shark onto the deck to take its skin, its jaws suddenly closed and almost cut off his hand. The shark was dead, but its body was still alive with hate. "Queequeg no care what god made him shark," said my friend, shaking his hand in pain. "Fiji god or Nantucket god — but the god that made shark must be a very bad god."

**Cutting In**

The next morning, the work began. This is called **cutting in**. The mates stood on a platform over the side of the ship, with long, sharp spades. They cut a hole in the whale's body, near the head, and pushed a huge iron hook into it. The hook was tied to a heavy rope that went up to the top of the mast.

Then the sailors pulled on the rope, singing, and the hook began to lift a long strip of the whale's thick skin and fat. As the strip went up, the whale turned round and round in the water, and the fat came off in a spiral, like the peel of an orange. This long, thick strip is called the **blanket piece**. It went up into the air and then down into a dark room below the deck, where men cut it into smaller pieces.

The whale's fat, or **blubber**, is about a foot thick. It keeps the whale warm in the coldest seas, like a thick coat. In the same way, a man should keep his own warmth inside him, whatever the weather outside. Be cool at the equator, warm at the poles, like the whale!

**The Funeral**

When all the blubber was off, the sailors cut the whale's head off, and let the white, naked body go. It floated away slowly, and birds and sharks came to eat it. It was like a funeral: the screaming birds were the mourners.

And even after its death, the whale frightened ships. Ships that saw the white body floating far away, with the birds over it, often thought that it was a rock or a dangerous reef. They wrote it on their charts, and for years afterwards, other ships sailed carefully around that empty place in the sea. That is how men's beliefs often work: they continue long after the truth has gone.

**The Head**

The great head of the whale hung over the side of the ship, held by chains. At noon, when the men had gone to eat, Ahab came and stood alone in front of it. He looked at it for a long time. Then he spoke.

"Speak, you great and ancient head," he said, "hanging there, without a beard. Tell us the secret thing that is in you. Of all divers, you have dived the deepest. You have seen the bottom of the sea, where sailors lie forgotten, and where ships have gone down. You have seen murdered men thrown into the sea, and ships sink with all their crews. Oh, head! You have seen enough to break the planets. And yet you are silent!"

"Sail ho!" cried a voice from the mast-head. Another ship was coming.

"Ah, that is good," said Ahab, standing up straight. "That lively cry would wake a dead man."

**The Jeroboam**

The ship was the *Jeroboam*, from Nantucket. She came close, and her captain, Mayhew, rowed over in a boat. But he did not come on board, because there was a terrible disease on his ship, and he did not want to give it to us. So he stood in his boat and talked from there.

In the boat with him was a strange young man with freckles and long yellow hair. He was wearing a long coat, and he looked like a religious fanatic. This was Gabriel. Stubb whispered to me that he had heard about him from other ships. He had been a member of a strange religious group in America. On board the *Jeroboam*, he had declared that he was the archangel Gabriel, and most of the sailors believed him and were afraid of him. He had so much power over them that the captain could not even punish him.

Ahab asked Captain Mayhew if he had seen Moby Dick.

Yes, they had. Some time ago, the *Jeroboam* had met the white whale. Gabriel had warned the crew not to attack him. "The white whale is the Shaker God," he said. But the mate, Macey, did not listen. He lowered his boat and went after the whale. As he stood up in the front of the boat to throw his lance, the whale rose up suddenly and hit him with its tail. Macey was thrown high into the air and fell into the sea, fifty yards away. He was dead. Nobody else in the boat was hurt.

"Do you intend to hunt the white whale?" Captain Mayhew asked Ahab.

"Yes," said Ahab.

"Think, think of the blasphemer!" cried Gabriel, pointing at Ahab. "Dead, and down there! Beware of the blasphemer's end!"

Ahab turned away. Then he remembered that there was a letter on the *Pequod* for someone on the *Jeroboam*. Ships often carry letters for other ships, hoping to meet them at sea. He found it, old and dirty. It was for Mr Macey, the dead mate — from his wife.

Ahab put the letter on the end of a long pole and held it out to the boat. But Gabriel took a knife, stuck it through the letter, and threw it back. It fell at Ahab's feet. "Nay, keep it thyself," cried Gabriel. "Thou art soon going that way!"

Then the boat rowed back to the *Jeroboam*, and Gabriel's wild laughter came back to us over the water.
