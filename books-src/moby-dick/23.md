## Part 23 — The Try-Works

**A Squeeze of the Hand**

When the spermaceti is taken from the whale's head and left in the air, it becomes hard, with small lumps in it. To make it smooth again, the sailors must sit around big tubs and squeeze the lumps with their hands until they become liquid.

I sat with several other sailors around a tub one sunny afternoon, squeezing spermaceti. It was the most pleasant work I ever did. The spermaceti was soft and warm, and it smelled sweet, like spring flowers. My hands became soft, and my heart became soft too. All the anger and bad feelings I had ever felt disappeared.

I squeezed and squeezed, until I found myself squeezing the hands of the other sailors by mistake, thinking they were lumps of spermaceti. And I felt such love for them! I looked into their eyes, and I wanted to say: "Oh, my dear fellow men, why should we ever be angry with each other? Let us all squeeze hands, and squeeze ourselves into each other, and become one in the milk of kindness."

I have learned that happiness is not found in great ideas or big dreams. It is found in simple things: your wife, your heart, your bed, your table, the saddle, the fireside, the country. If I could, I would squeeze spermaceti for ever.

**The Try-Works**

On the deck of a whaling ship, between the masts, there is a strange structure made of bricks, like a small factory. These are the **try-works** — two huge ovens with two big iron pots on top, where the blubber is boiled to make oil.

When a whale has been cut in, the fat is cut into small pieces, and the pieces are thrown into the pots. The fire is made with the dry, crispy pieces of fat that are left after boiling — so the whale burns its own body to make its own oil. The smoke is black and smells terrible, like a funeral pyre.

At night, the try-works are a wild sight. The fires burn red in the darkness. The harpooners stand around them, with their faces lit by the flames, stirring the boiling oil with long forks, telling terrible stories and laughing. Their faces look like devils'. The ship rushes through the black night with her red fire burning, like a ship from hell.

One night, I was at the helm, steering the ship, while the try-works were burning. I looked into the fire for a long time, and I became sleepy. I closed my eyes for a moment. When I opened them, I was confused. I could not see the compass. Everything around me was black, except the red fire. I felt that the ship was running backwards, away from her direction. Then I understood: I had turned round in my sleep, and I was facing the back of the ship! I was holding the helm the wrong way, and the ship was turning dangerously against the wind. In another moment she would have turned over! I quickly turned round and corrected her course.

From that night I learned a lesson: do not look too long into the fire. Do not let artificial light deceive you. Tomorrow the natural sun will show you the truth. The sun hides neither the sea nor the dark side of the earth, which is two thirds of it. A man who sees only joy is a fool, but a man who sees only sadness is also lost. There is a wisdom that is sadness, and there is a sadness that is madness. Be careful not to let it take you.

**The Lamp**

On a whaling ship, there is always light. On merchant ships, the sailors sleep in the dark, because oil is expensive. But on a whaling ship, there is so much oil that the sailors can burn lamps all night. The whaleman lives in light, like a king.

**Clearing Up**

When the oil is boiled and cooled, it is poured into big barrels, called **casks**, and stored deep down in the ship. Then the whole ship is cleaned. The sailors wash the decks with soap and water, and scrub everything until it shines. The ship looks as clean as a new house. The men put on fresh clothes, and they look like brides.

But they have hardly finished when the cry comes again from the mast-head: "There she blows!" And the boats are lowered again, and the whole bloody, dirty, smoky work begins once more. Such is the life of a whaleman. Every time we finish, we must start again.
