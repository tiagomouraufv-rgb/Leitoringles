## Part 13 — The Spirit-Spout and the Albatross

Days and weeks passed. The *Pequod* sailed slowly south through the Atlantic, past the islands of the Azores, past the coast of Africa, towards the Cape of Good Hope.

**The Spirit-Spout**

One clear night, when the moon was full and the sea was like silver, Fedallah was standing at the main mast-head. Suddenly he cried out in a strange voice that sounded almost like a ghost's: "There she blows!"

Far away, in the moonlight, there was a silvery jet of water, rising and falling. It was a whale's spout — but a strange one. It looked like a ghost on the water.

Everyone on the ship ran to look. Ahab ordered the men to put up every sail, and the *Pequod* rushed through the night towards the spout. But the spout was not seen again that night.

A few nights later, the same silvery spout appeared again in the moonlight, far ahead of the ship. And again, it disappeared when we came closer. This happened night after night. It was always in front of us, always far away. It seemed to be calling us, leading us on.

The sailors began to whisper that it was a spirit — or that it was Moby Dick himself, leading us further and further into wild and dangerous seas, so that he could turn and destroy us there.

After the Cape of Good Hope, the sea became wild and stormy. The ship rolled and pitched in the enormous waves. Black sea birds flew around us, screaming. Every morning they sat on the ropes, as if they thought our ship was an empty, abandoned wreck. Through all those terrible days, Ahab stood on the deck in the wind and rain. When the storm was very strong, he tied himself to the ship with ropes, so that the waves would not wash him away. He hardly spoke, and he never smiled. He only looked out over the stormy sea, with his eyes full of his terrible purpose.

**The Albatross**

South-east of the Cape, the *Pequod* met another ship — a whaling ship called the *Albatross*. She was on her way home, after more than four years at sea.

She looked like a ghost. Her sides were white, bleached by the sun and the salt water, like the bones of a dead walrus. Long, grey seaweed hung from her. At her mast-heads stood men with long beards, dressed in skins. They looked at us without moving, like old prophets. They did not seem to see us at all.

As the two ships came close, Ahab shouted with his trumpet, "Ship ahoy! Have you seen the White Whale?"

The other captain raised his trumpet to answer, but at that moment it fell from his hand into the sea. He tried to shout without it, but the wind took his voice away. We could not hear his answer.

At the same moment, a strange thing happened. Small fish that had been swimming peacefully next to the *Pequod* for many days suddenly turned and swam away to the *Albatross*.

"Swim away from me, do you?" said Ahab sadly, looking at the water. The ships passed each other and sailed away in opposite directions.

Then Ahab shouted to the other ship: "This is the *Pequod*, going round the world! Tell them at home to send all future letters to the Pacific Ocean! And if I do not come home in three years, tell them to send letters to—" But the other ship was already too far away to hear.

"Round the world!" I thought. "We sail round and round the world, but where are we going? To find the same troubles we left behind? To chase a dream that runs away from us, or a phantom that leads us to our death?"

**Meetings at Sea**

The *Pequod* did not stop to talk with the *Albatross*, and this surprised me. Usually, when two whaling ships meet at sea, they stop and the crews visit each other. This visit is called a **gam**. The captains go on board one ship, and the mates go on board the other. They exchange news from home, letters, and information about where the whales are. It is a happy, friendly custom. Far from land, in the empty ocean, whalemen are like brothers.

But Ahab did not want news from home. He only wanted news of the white whale. And since the *Albatross* could not tell him about Moby Dick, he did not want to stop.
