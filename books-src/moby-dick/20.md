## Part 20 — The Virgin and More About Whales

**The Virgin**

One day we met a German whaling ship called the *Jungfrau*, which means "the Virgin". Her captain, Derick De Deer, rowed over to us in his boat. He was holding something in his hand. When he came closer, we saw that it was an oil can.

"Look at him!" laughed Stubb. "He's coming to beg for oil!"

It was true. The German ship had not caught a single whale on its voyage. It had no oil left, not even for its lamps. The captain asked politely if we could give him some. Starbuck filled his can, and the German was very grateful.

But just as he was going back to his ship, the men at the mast-heads of both ships shouted at the same time: "Whales!" A group of eight whales was swimming not far away. Captain De Deer forgot his oil can and rowed back to his ship as fast as he could. Then the boats of both ships were lowered, and there was a race.

The Germans were ahead at first. But our men rowed harder and harder. Among the whales was a big, old one, who swam slowly and strangely, because one of his fins was missing. He was blowing a weak, short spout. "Who's got some paregoric?" laughed Stubb. "He has a stomach ache!"

The three boats of the *Pequod* — Starbuck's, Stubb's and Flask's — passed the German boat. All three harpooners threw their harpoons into the old whale at the same moment. The whale dived deep, pulling all three boats after him. Then he came up again, tired and slow. The mates killed him with their lances. The poor old whale had been blind and sick, and he died sadly.

When the men began to cut into his body, they found something strange: the point of an old stone harpoon, deep in his flesh, completely covered by new skin. It must have been there for many years — maybe since before America was discovered! Someone had hunted this whale long, long ago, and he had survived.

But then the whale began to sink. He was so heavy that he pulled the ship down to one side, and we had to cut the chains to let him go. He sank to the bottom of the sea, and all our work was lost.

Meanwhile, the German ship had seen another spout, and its boats were chasing it. But it was a fin-back whale, which is too fast to catch. We watched them row after it for a long time, getting further and further away, with no hope of success.

**The Honour of Whaling**

Whaling is one of the oldest and most noble professions in the world. The first whaleman, I believe, was Perseus, the Greek hero, who killed a sea monster to save the beautiful Andromeda. Saint George, who killed the dragon, was probably also a whaleman: the "dragon" was really a whale. Hercules was swallowed by a whale, like Jonah, and came out alive. Even the Indian god Vishnu, the old books say, once took the form of a whale. So whalemen belong to a great family of heroes and gods.

**Jonah**

Some people say that the story of Jonah cannot be true. How could a man live inside a whale for three days? An old sailor once told me that perhaps Jonah was not inside the whale at all, but inside a ship called *The Whale*. Others say that Jonah hid in the dead body of a whale. But I believe the Bible.

**The Javelin**

Sometimes, when a whale swims too fast for the boats to come close, the mate throws his lance from far away, like a javelin. This is called **pitchpoling**. Stubb was the best at it. He threw his long lance high into the air, and it came down like a bird into the whale's back. Then he pulled it back with the rope tied to it, and threw it again, and again, until the whale was dead.

**The Spout**

What comes out of a whale's head when it spouts? Many people think it is water. But I believe that it is only breath — warm, wet air, like the steam from a kettle. Nobody knows for sure, because nobody has been close enough to touch it without danger. Some sailors say that if the spout touches your skin, it burns. Some say that if it gets into your eyes, it makes you blind.

When I see the great white spout rising from the whale's head, I think it is like the misty thoughts of a deep thinker. I believe that a whale is always thinking. It seems to me that the mist over his head is like the cloud that rises from the head of a philosopher, deep in thought. And sometimes, in the spout, there is a rainbow, which only shines on such clouds of thought — as if heaven itself were blessing his mind.

**The Tail**

The tail of the sperm whale is the most beautiful and powerful thing in nature. It is about twenty feet wide, flat and smooth. It can move in five different ways: to swim forward, to fight, to sweep the water around it, to slap the surface, and to rise high into the air as the whale dives down. With one hit of his tail, a whale can throw a boat and all its men high into the air. And yet, he can also use it very gently, to touch the water like a hand. In his tail, the whale has both great power and great grace. The more I think about it, the less I understand it. If I cannot even understand his tail, how can I understand his head? And how can I understand the whole whale?
