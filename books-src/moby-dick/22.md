## Part 22 — The Rose-bud and Pip

**Whale Families**

Sperm whales live in two kinds of groups. Some groups are made of females with their young ones, led by one big, strong male, like a sultan with his wives. He is very jealous. If another male comes near, there is a terrible fight. But when he grows old, he leaves the group and lives alone, grumpy and wise. The other kind of group is made of young males, who are wild and quarrelsome, like students. When they are hunted, they do not help each other. Each one runs away alone.

**Fast-Fish and Loose-Fish**

Whalemen have a simple law about who owns a whale. If a whale is tied by a rope to a boat or a ship, it is a **Fast-Fish**, and it belongs to the people who are holding the rope. If it is not tied to anything, it is a **Loose-Fish**, and anyone can take it. This law is very simple, but it can explain the whole world. What is a slave but a Fast-Fish? What is America, in 1492, but a Loose-Fish, taken by Columbus for Spain? And what are you, reader, but a Loose-Fish and a Fast-Fish too?

**The Rose-bud**

A few weeks later, we noticed a terrible smell coming over the sea. Soon we saw a French whaling ship, the *Rose-bud*. Tied to her sides were two dead whales — very old and rotten. One of them had died of disease, and the other had been found dead in the sea. They gave almost no oil, and they smelled horrible.

"Ah," said Stubb, holding his nose. "A rose-bud! What a sweet smell!" He looked at the rotten whales with a clever look in his eyes. "I think there might be something valuable in one of them," he said quietly. "These French don't know anything about whaling."

He rowed over to the French ship. The captain did not speak English, but the mate, who came from the island of Guernsey, did. The mate hated his captain and wanted to get rid of the stinking whales. So Stubb and the mate made a plan. Stubb told the mate to tell his captain that the rotten whales were very dangerous, and that they could give the whole crew a terrible fever.

The mate translated, but not exactly what Stubb said. He told the captain that the American gentleman had warned them about the danger of disease. The French captain was very frightened. He thanked Stubb, ordered his men to cut the whales loose at once, and sailed away.

As soon as the French ship had gone, Stubb rowed to the sick whale, which was now a Loose-Fish, and began to dig into its body with a spade. The smell was terrible. The men thought he was mad. But after some time, a sweet, strong perfume came up from the rotten whale. Stubb shouted with joy. He pulled out handfuls of a soft, grey substance like wax. It was **ambergris** — one of the most valuable things in the world!

He had taken six handfuls when Ahab called the boat back to the ship. Stubb would have taken more, but he had to obey.

**Ambergris**

Ambergris is found inside the bodies of sick sperm whales. It is used to make the finest perfumes, and it costs more than gold. Isn't it strange that the most beautiful perfume comes from the stomach of a sick, rotten whale? It is like the Bible, which says that the bodies of the dead will rise again in glory. Out of the worst things, sometimes the best things come.

**Pip**

Now I must tell you the sad story of little Pip.

Pip had always stayed on the ship when the boats were lowered, because he was only a boy. But one day, one of the men in Stubb's boat hurt his hand, and Pip had to take his place.

The first time, Pip was very frightened. When Tashtego's harpoon hit a whale and the whale hit the boat with its tail, Pip jumped out of the boat in terror. He got caught in the line, which was running out after the whale, and it began to choke him. Tashtego had to cut the line to save him, and they lost the whale.

Stubb was angry. "Stick to the boat, Pip," he said. "Or by the Lord, I won't pick you up if you jump again. We can't afford to lose whales for the likes of you. A whale would sell for thirty times what you would, Pip, in Alabama. Remember that, and don't jump any more."

But the next time, Pip jumped again. This time there was no line around him, and Stubb kept his promise. The boat went on after the whale and left Pip alone in the middle of the ocean.

It was a beautiful, calm day. The sea was smooth and blue, and it went on for ever in all directions. Pip floated alone, his little black head like a dark spot in the enormous sea. He watched the boats getting smaller and smaller until they disappeared. The loneliness was terrible.

Stubb had not really intended to leave him for long. He thought the other boats would pick him up. But the other boats were busy chasing whales too, and nobody saw him. At last, by chance, the *Pequod* itself found him and saved him.

But from that hour, Pip was never the same. He walked around the ship like an idiot. People said he was mad. The sea had kept his body alive, but it had drowned his soul. He had gone down to the bottom of the ocean in his mind, and there he had seen strange things: the wonders of the deep, the beginning of the world, the foot of God. And men call that madness. But perhaps his madness was the wisdom of heaven. What man calls madness, heaven may call wisdom.
